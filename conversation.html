Step 3

Conversation and follow-up

The food is the backdrop. The real goal of the meal is a good conversation and a relationship that continues afterward.

Start with small talk

Dinner is a social occasion, so ease in with light topics: travel, food, movies, sports, or what brought each of you to your field. Ask open questions and listen to the answers.

Business usually comes up later, often after the main course, and your host will normally steer toward it. Follow their lead instead of pushing your agenda.

Sources: Business Management Daily, NBC News (Dizik).

Be easy to talk to

Sit up straight, keep your phone away, and finish chewing before you answer. If you are asked something while your mouth is full, a small nod buys you a moment.

Be warm toward the servers too. Your host will notice how you treat them, even when service is slow.

Sources: Business Management Daily, NBC News (Dizik), The US at Work (Kaye).

Drinks

Let your host set the tone. If they order wine and you are of age and want some, one glass is plenty. If you are under 21, or simply do not drink, water, soda, or iced tea is completely fine and no one will mind.

Sources: NBC News (Dizik).

The bill

The person who invited you normally pays, and a considerate host often settles up quietly so no one has to look at a check. As a guest, do not grab the bill or ask the total. A sincere thank-you is the right reply.

Leave the tip to your host as well. You would need to see the full bill to calculate it, so offering to cover it can create confusion.

Sources: NBC News (Dizik), American Nurse Journal (Pagana).

Follow up

Within a day or two, send a short thank-you by email or on paper. Mention something specific from the conversation, such as an idea you liked or advice you received.

Then do whatever you promised, like sending a link or making an introduction. Following through is what turns a pleasant dinner into a professional connection.

Sources: NBC News (Dizik).

Conversation starters you can borrow

  • "How did you get started in this field?"
  • "What is a project you are excited about right now?"
  • "Have you traveled anywhere interesting recently?"
  • "What do you wish you had known when you were a student?"
  • "Is there anything here you would recommend?"
