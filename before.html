Step 1

Before the meal

Most of the work happens before you sit down. A little preparation makes the rest feel easy.

Get ready before you sit down.

1

Research your host

Spend ten minutes learning about the person you are meeting and the organization they work for. A quick online search is enough. You will have better questions to ask, and you will not look surprised when their job title comes up.

Look for common ground too, such as a hobby, a hometown, or a project you both care about. It gives you something real to talk about.

Sources: Business Management Daily, NBC News (Dizik).

2

Think about the restaurant

If you are the one choosing, aim for the middle: a place that feels too casual or cheap can suggest you do not value your guest, and one that is very expensive can look wasteful. If you are unsure, it is perfectly fine to let the other person pick.

Browsing a menu in advance helps, because you can decide what to order before you arrive. Sites such as OpenTable (opens in a new tab) show menus, prices, and reservation options.

Sources: Business Management Daily.

3

Plan your arrival

Get there before your guest, or at least on time. Arriving early lets you ask for a quieter table away from busy walkways, and it shows the other person that their time matters to you.

Not sure what to wear? Ask the host or the recruiter. When you cannot ask, dress one step more formal than you think the restaurant requires.

Sources: NBC News (Dizik).

4

Prepare a few topics

Bring two or three easy subjects, such as travel, movies, sports, or local food, so silences never feel awkward. Think of one or two questions about your host's work as well.

Finally, silence your phone and put it where you will not see it. If you have a food allergy or dietary need, let your host know ahead of time rather than at the table.

Sources: Business Management Daily.

Your pre-meal checklist

  • I looked up my host and what their organization does.
  • I know where the restaurant is, how I will get there, and when I need to leave.
  • I have two or three conversation topics and a couple of questions ready.
  • I have an outfit that is clean, comfortable, and a step more formal than casual.
  • My phone is on silent, and I have told my host about any dietary needs.
